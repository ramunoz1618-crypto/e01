What is term life insurance?

Term life: what to know

Pays a death benefitIf the insured passes away during the term.
Generally no cash valueIt provides protection only.
StrengthUsually the lowest cost for the amount of coverage.
LimitationCoverage may end when the term does.
Options to ask about
RenewalConversion to permanent coverageReturn of premium (ROP)

ROP costs more in premium in exchange for a refund of premiums if you outlive the term.

How whole life works

Whole life: strengths and trade-offs

Death benefit and cash value

Permanent coverageDesigned to last your lifetime if premiums are paid.
Fixed premiumThe amount you pay does not normally change.
Guaranteed death benefitAs long as the required premiums are paid.
Cash valueGrows according to the policy's terms.
Strengths
  • Predictability
  • Lifetime coverage
  • Guaranteed elements
Trade-offs
  • Higher premiums than term
  • Cash value builds gradually, so early access is limited
  • Little flexibility to change premiums
  • Dividends (on participating policies) are not guaranteed

Related: final expense insurance is a smaller whole life policy designed for end-of-life costs.

Cash value is generally part of the policy, not an add-on to the death benefit. The exact payout depends on the policy and any loans.

How universal life works

Universal life: flexibility and risks

Premium goes inYour payment goes into the policy.
Cost of insurance comes outEach month, it is taken from the cash value.
The rest stays and earns interestWhat's left remains in the cash value.
Flexibility
  • Adjustable premiums
  • Adjustable death benefit
Risks
  • Cash value is generally not guaranteed
  • Underfunding can cause the policy to lapse
  • Costs are higher than term

Related: guaranteed universal life focuses on a guaranteed death benefit rather than cash value.

Indexed universal life: strengths and trade-offs

Three ways cash value can be credited

What is indexing?

Strengths
  • Potential for growth, with protection from index losses
  • Cash value access through loans and withdrawals, which may have tax advantages if structured properly
Trade-offs
  • Higher cost than term
  • Growth is not guaranteed and is limited by caps
  • Policy charges still apply, and loans can cause a lapse if mismanaged
  • Illustrations are hypothetical, not promises
Fixed accountA stated interest rate.
Indexed accountGrowth linked to an index, with limits on the upside and a floor on the downside.
Variable accountMarket exposure with gain and loss potential. Requires a securities license to discuss (VUL).
Interest based on an indexAn IUL is a universal life policy with interest credited based on an index's performance.
Not invested directly in the marketYour cash value is not placed in stocks or an index fund.
Common terms
  • Cap: the maximum rate that can be credited
  • Participation rate: the share of the index gain that counts
  • Floor: the minimum credit, often 0%

The four types at a glance

TermMost affordable, but time-limitedCoverage for a set period, generally with no cash value.
Whole lifeLifetime coverage, fixed and less flexibleFixed premium and gradual cash value, with limited control.
Universal lifeFlexible, with cash value that can help pay for coverageAdjust premiums and death benefit. Cash value can cover the cost of insurance.
Indexed universal lifeUniversal life with index-linked growthDeath benefit plus cash value. A 0% floor protects against index losses, but growth is capped.

Each one is designed for a different need. A licensed agent can help you find what fits your situation.
